Yes. I think there is a genuinely interesting product idea here.

The key is **not to make text behave like video**. It's to ask what makes short video frictionless and rewarding, then reproduce those properties using text.

Short video has a loop like:

> **instant stimulus → tiny payoff → tiny interaction → completion → next**

Normal Twitter-style text is more like:

> paragraph → read → maybe like → scroll

There is almost no interaction **during** consumption. The post is basically a dead rectangle.

So I'd explore **interactive text as a first-class media format**.

# 1. “Text Reels” — but actually designed for reading

Imagine an optional NSosyal feed called something like **Akış / Hızlı Oku**.

One post occupies most of the viewport:

> ### Mars'ta bugün ilginç bir şey oldu.
>
> Perseverance'ın topladığı örneklerden biri daha önce görülmeyen bir mineral yapısı içeriyor.
>
> **Neden önemli? ↓**

Tap **Neden önemli?**

The card smoothly expands:

> Çünkü bu mineral yalnızca uzun süre sıvı su bulunan ortamlarda oluşabiliyor.

Then:

**Kaynak** · **Daha teknik anlat** · **Karşı görüş** · **Kaydet**

Swipe up.

Next post.

You're still reading **actual writing**, but there's a much tighter:

> read → interact → reward → continue

loop.

---

# 2. The most important idea: **progressive text**

Don't dump the entire post onto the screen immediately.

Allow authors to deliberately create **reveals**.

For example:

> Türkiye'nin en fazla ihracat yaptığı ülke hangisi?

**Tahmin et ↓**

Buttons:

`Almanya` `ABD` `İtalya` `İngiltere`

Tap.

> **Almanya.**
>
> Ama daha ilginç olan şu:
>
> Son 10 yılda payı...

**Devam et**

That's text, yet suddenly it has **rhythm**.

And crucially, you could apply this to:

- explanations
- jokes
- stories
- quizzes
- news
- education
- trivia
- arguments
- tutorials
- mysteries
- sports
- statistics.

It gives the writer control over **pacing**, something conventional social text barely supports.

---

# 3. Inline “tell me more” buttons

This might be the killer mechanic.

Authors don't have to choose between:

> ridiculously short tweet

and

> enormous wall of text.

They can make pieces optionally expandable.

Example:

> Turkey's inflation methodology changed in 2025.  
> **[What changed?]**
>
> This altered how X was calculated.  
> **[Show example]**
>
> Critics argue Y.  
> **[Why?]**
>
> TÜİK's response is Z.  
> **[Their argument]**

The reader determines depth.

A casual reader consumes it in **eight seconds**.

Someone interested can spend **three minutes** inside the same post.

That's really powerful.

Call them something like:

### **Katmanlar — Layers**

The post has depth without visually looking long.

---

# 4. Sentence-level interaction

Why is the atomic interaction unit always the entire post?

Imagine tapping/holding one sentence:

> The proposal would increase the threshold from 20% to 30%.

Then:

**❤️** **?** **!** **Kaynak?** **Yanıtla**

You could see:

> 284 people found this sentence important  
> 61 requested a source  
> 17 replies specifically address this claim

That would make long writing **feel alive while you're reading it**.

And it solves another problem: replies become anchored to **what the person is actually replying to**.

Instead of:

> “Wrong.”

under a 900-word article.

You get:

> Mehmet replied specifically to paragraph 4, sentence 2.

That's fantastic for serious discussion.

---

# 5. Tap-to-explain anything

This could involve Social AI without becoming the dreaded:

> ✨ Write my post with AI ✨

Hold a phrase:

> **quantitative tightening**

Tiny menu:

**Açıkla**  
**Çevir**  
**Bağlam**  
**Kaynak**

Tap **Açıkla**:

> **Quantitative tightening**
>
> A central bank reduces the amount of money/liquidity in the financial system, usually by shrinking its balance sheet.
>
> `Dismiss`

You never leave the feed.

That attacks one of text's biggest friction points:

> **“I don't understand this thing, therefore I'm leaving/skipping.”**

Video often explains context automatically because a human is talking to you.

Interactive text could do the same **on demand**.

---

# 6. The “Why?” button

This could become almost an NSosyal signature.

Any declarative post can expose:

**Neden?**  
**Kaynak?**  
**Örnek?**  
**Tersi?**

Example:

> Solar is now the cheapest source of new electricity generation in many markets.

Tap:

### **Kaynak?**

Sources slide out.

Tap:

### **Tersi?**

> Important qualifications:
> grid storage, geography, transmission and intermittency can substantially change system-level cost.

Tap:

### **Örnek?**

A real example appears.

This gives information **handles**.

Right now text is passive. You either accept it, ignore it, or manually search elsewhere.

# 7. Tiny interactive objects inside posts

Authors could embed little **microblocks**.

Not enormous widgets. Tiny things.

### Prediction

> Will Galatasaray win tonight?

`Yes 64%` `No 36%`

Tap once and continue reading.

### Slider

> How strongly do you agree?

`←────●────→`

### Reveal

> Guess the answer.

`Reveal`

### Ranking

> Which matters most?

Drag:

1. Economy
2. Education
3. Immigration

### Before/after

Slide horizontally.

### Timeline

`2022 ●── 2023 ●── 2024 ●── 2025`

Tap a year.

### Map/location

Tap the mentioned place.

### Tiny chart

Touch a datapoint.

### Citation

Tap `[1]`, source preview floats up.

All without leaving the post.

That's where text starts becoming something **video fundamentally cannot do**.

---

# 8. “Choose your depth”

This one I really like.

Every substantial post could expose:

**10 sec** · **30 sec** · **2 min**

Same underlying content.

### 10 sec

> Scientists found evidence suggesting X.

### 30 sec

Three-paragraph explanation.

### 2 min

Full version with methods, evidence and sources.

This doesn't necessarily have to be AI-generated. Authors could structure it themselves, with AI optionally helping derive the shorter layers.

The reader says:

> **How much attention do I want to spend on this?**

Instead of the platform deciding for them.

That fits our entire **user-control** thesis beautifully.

---

# 9. “Continue when interested”

Instead of showing a thread as:

> 1/17  
> 2/17  
> 3/17  
> 4/17...

make it one interactive object.

Initially:

> I spent six months investigating why İstanbul's buses bunch together.

Then:

### **Show me ↓**

Next paragraph animates in.

> The obvious explanation is traffic.
>
> It's mostly wrong.

### **Why? ↓**

Continue.

Now you've created the text equivalent of **retention editing** without requiring a video.

Good writers already do this rhetorically.

The interface would simply **give rhetoric a physical form**.

---

# 10. Reward completion, not scrolling

I'd be very careful here.

We don't want:

> +5 XP for reading propaganda!

But subtle completion feedback works.

For a substantial post:

`● ● ● ○ ○`

As you move through its layers.

At the end:

> ✓ **Read completely**

And perhaps:

> 81% of readers reached this point.

Or privately:

> **12 min read today**
>
> Technology · History · Engineering

You're rewarding:

> **I understood/finished something**

rather than:

> I watched another 73 videos.

That's potentially a healthier engagement metric.

---

# 11. Make replies themselves explorable

Typical replies are a disaster.

Imagine a post has 1,800 replies.

Instead:

### Conversation

**Questions** 142  
**Disagreements** 87  
**Supporting evidence** 34  
**Jokes** 614  
**Author replies** 19

Tap **Disagreements**.

Then perhaps:

> **Three recurring counterarguments**

1. Methodology problem
2. Missing economic context
3. Alternative interpretation

And then the *actual human replies* underneath.

AI isn't replacing discussion.

It's helping the reader **navigate it**.

This could make text discussions vastly faster to consume.

# 12. Branching discussions

Instead of every thread collapsing into one giant reply tree:

> Post
>
> **What happened? →**
>
> **Why does it matter? →**
>
> **Is this true? →**
>
> **What happens next? →**
>
> **Opinions →**

Readers choose the branch.

It's basically **hypertext finally being used properly in social media**.

The web invented links thirty years ago and somehow social feeds devolved back into rectangles of text.

# 14. One-thumb reading

This addresses your “effort” point.

Make virtually everything operable with the thumb:

**Tap**
> reveal

**Hold**
> context/explain

**Swipe left**
> discussion

**Swipe right**
> save

**Swipe up**
> next post

No opening twenty screens.

No tiny links.

No navigating away.

If interaction takes **500 ms**, it feels playful.

If it takes five seconds and opens a modal, it feels like work.

---

# 15. Text could actually beat video on speed

This is the important strategic insight.

We shouldn't try to make text **as stimulating as TikTok**.

Text has an advantage TikTok doesn't:

> **humans can scan text extremely quickly.**

You can understand:

> **ECB cuts rates by 0.25 percentage points**

in perhaps a second.

A video has to spend ten seconds saying:

> “So guys, some pretty big news coming out of Europe today…”

So the product should exploit text's actual advantage:

### extremely high information density

combined with:

### optional interaction when something catches you.

The formula becomes:

> **scan → notice → tap → understand → continue**

rather than:

> **watch → wait → watch → wait → swipe**

That's potentially better than competing directly with video.

# An actual product concept emerges

I'd tentatively call the underlying format:

# **Living Text**

or, in Turkish perhaps something around:

### **Canlı Metin**
### **Akış Kartları**
### **Katmanlı Gönderi**
### **Dokun-Oku**
### **Metin+**

The important thing isn't the name.

The content model would be something like:

```text id="hexg2v"
Post
 ├── Hook
 ├── Paragraph
 │    ├── Explain
 │    ├── Source
 │    └── Discussion
 ├── Reveal
 ├── Poll
 ├── Paragraph
 │    └── Counterpoint
 └── Conclusion
```

A post stops being:

```text id="00ap4o"
string body;
image[];
```

and becomes a sequence of **interactive semantic blocks**.

That's actually a fairly significant architectural idea.

## And it fits the competition unusually well

It sits in **User Engagement + UI/UX**, obviously.

But if you add:

- AI explanations,
- contextual definitions,
- reply clustering,
- optional depth generation,

it also touches **Social AI**.

And unlike:

> “we made TikTok but Turkish”

the pitch becomes:

> **Short-form video optimized the feed for passive audiovisual consumption. We asked what the equivalent innovation would look like if the native medium were text.**

That is a **much better research question**.

I would absolutely add **“interactive / living text” as a new project family** in our matrix. I don't know yet whether it should win, but this is one of the more original directions we've surfaced.
