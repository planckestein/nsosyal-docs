Yes. And after a proper pass, **Russia is absolutely worth mining**, although for somewhat different reasons than China.

One important correction to the premise: VK, Odnoklassniki, Dzen and RUTUBE all existed well before 2022. What the post-2022 environment appears to have accelerated is **domestic ecosystem consolidation**—messaging, video, payments, identity, government services, creator monetization and business tools increasingly being pulled into locally controlled platforms. The most interesting new example is **MAX**, launched in 2025 as Russia's “national messenger” and already being turned into something closer to a digital-services layer. ([vk.company.ru](https://vk.company.ru/ru/press/releases/12255/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

This is the standout.

([help.max.ru](https://help.max.ru/help/account/kak-sozdat-czifrovoj-id-v-max?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

- proving certain statuses,
- checking into hotels,
- business verification. ([vk.company.ru](https://vk.company.ru/ru/press/releases/12292/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That gives us a much more interesting version of the verification idea we discussed earlier.

## **NSosyal Credentials**

Instead of merely:

> ✓ Verified

a person could selectively prove:

**✓ Over 18**  
**✓ University student**  
**✓ ITÜ student**  
**✓ Licensed physician**  
**✓ Registered company representative**

without making their whole identity document public.

The ideal NSosyal version should be privacy-preserving:

> **prove the attribute, not expose the document.**

So instead of handing a community your birth date:

> `age >= 18 → TRUE`

Instead of publishing your entire student ID:

> `ITU_student → TRUE`

That could be enormously useful for communities.

Imagine:

Public can still read.

That attacks impersonation and creates **high-trust communities without requiring every user to display their legal identity publicly.**

# 14. RUTUBE has a bizarre creator behavioral metric worth studying

This is where Russia intersects with our previous dystopian research.

RUTUBE formally defines something called the **Индивидуальный Медиаиндикатор Поведенческой Активности — IMBA/ИМПА**, roughly an *Individual Media Indicator of Behavioral Activity*. ([rutube.ru](https://rutube.ru/info/impa_agreement_no/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Its documentation says the metric can incorporate things including:

- average time spent,
- average watch time,
- completion percentage,
- inbound citation/linking,
- popularity of referring sources,
- subscriber activity,
- likes/dislikes/favorites,
- agreement compliance,
- external search-engine indexing,
- other indicators selected by the platform. ([rutube.ru](https://rutube.ru/info/adv_oferta_glossary/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

And high IMBA values can affect promotion/recommendation mechanisms. ([rutube.ru](https://rutube.ru/info/advpr_agreement/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That is fascinating because it is effectively:

> **a platform-defined creator/content health score.**

The benign NSosyal version could expose this *transparently*:

### Content Health

**Real engagement:** 91  
**Read completion:** 84  
**Originality:** 97  
**Discussion quality:** 73  
**Follower retention:** 88

Rather than the platform hiding the score.

The dystopian version, obviously, is exactly what we just spent the last turn discussing:

> secret score controls visibility.

So this one belongs in **both documents**.

## The really interesting difference between the regions

After China + Russia, I think we have a useful taxonomy:

**American platforms:**

> How do we make content spread?

**Chinese platforms:**

> How do we make the social network encompass everyday economic/social life?

**Russian platforms:**

> How do we connect the domestic social network to the country's own identity, institutions, businesses, media and services?

That last one is **very relevant to NSosyal**, because NSosyal's position as a Turkish domestic platform creates opportunities that Threads or Bluesky don't naturally have.

And one idea now jumps way up my list:

> **Verified Communities + selective credentials + actionable community services.**

For example, an **ITÜ community** where your student status is cryptographically verified, clubs have official pages, events have real RSVP, workshops have registration, discussions have student/faculty roles, and services run as mini-apps—*without publishing your legal ID to everyone*.

That takes pieces from **MAX + VK + BAND + LINE + WeChat**, but the resulting thing is no longer a clone of any one of them.

That is exactly the kind of synthesis I think we should be hunting for.
